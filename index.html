Optimization-guided Safe Reinforcement Learning for Privacy-Preserving Edge Service Placement

Secure edge service placement aims to assign multiple services to a limited number of Edge Computing Units (ECUs) while satisfying capacity and privacy-conflict constraints and improving the average resource utilization of activated ECUs. In this paper, we formulate the secure service placement problem as a constrained optimization problem and further extend it into a reinforcement learning (RL)-based framework to reduce computational complexity. To effectively enforce hard constraints in the RL environment, action masking is introduced. Under a unified custom environment and a common reward function, several constraint-aware mechanisms are compared, including an unconstrained baseline, Lagrangian-based soft constraints, action masking, and heuristic-based action repair, in combination with PPO, DQN, and DDQN. Experimental results show that the soft-constraint approach cannot reliably prevent constraint violations, whereas action masking and action repair significantly improve the probability of obtaining feasible placements. In particular, the action-masking-based RL method satisfies both capacity and privacy constraints while achieving approximately 10 times faster inference than the optimization-based method, at the cost of a certain optimality gap in resource utilization. These results demonstrate that action-masking-based reinforcement learning can provide a practical trade-off between constraint satisfaction and fast decision-making for secure edge service placement. Privacy-preserving service placement, safe reinforcement learning, reinforcement learning, integer linear programming, action masking, constrained optimization.
